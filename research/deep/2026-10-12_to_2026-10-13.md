### Direct code findings (main as inspected at start of run)
1. **[B]** `clsplats/lifter/depth_anything_lifter.py`: per camera, `visible_views[g]` increments once if g appears in *positive* kNN and again if it appears in *negative* kNN. Thus `visible_views[g]` can be 2 for **one camera** and up to `2 * valid_views`, despite the name. `positive_views[g]` increments at most once per positive-supported camera. `positive_ratio = positive_views / visible_views` is not a fraction of physically visible cameras, and can be artificially halved. `min_visible_views=2` can be satisfied from just one camera's positive+negative sampled pixels. `min_positive_views=2` still requires two positive-supported cameras for final retention. No independent projection-based visibility test appears in this evidence accumulator.
2. **[B]** Positive neighbors require local-scale distance AND `|z_gaussian-z_pixel| < depth_tol_abs + depth_tol_rel*d_pixel`; negative neighbors require only local-scale distance. This asymmetric depth gate can attach negative evidence to occluded/depth-inconsistent Gaussians. Impact on active-mask recall remains **unmeasured**.
3. **[B]** `estimate_depth` uses `self._pipe(pil_img)["depth"]` (PIL visualization), converts to float and divides by 255. The official Hugging Face depth-estimation pipeline supplies a distinct `predicted_depth` tensor and explicitly normalizes/quantizes its `depth` visualization to uint8; this means production lifter throws away float precision and introduces per-image 8-bit quantization **before metric disparity alignment**. Official source: https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/pipelines/depth_estimation.py and https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/docs/source/en/tasks/monocular_depth_estimation.md . An affine alignment can absorb image-wise scale/offset, but cannot undo quantization. **Potentially important additional bottleneck**.
4. **[B]** HF `pipeline("depth-estimation", model=...)` is created with no explicit `device`. Hugging Face pipeline docs say default device is CPU (-1) in supported versions, but actual installed runtime/device must be measured before claiming it runs on CPU. DINO detector separately uses CUDA when available. This could be a substantial latency bottleneck if depth inference defaults to CPU.
5. **[B]** Each valid view samples up to 2,048 positive and 1,024 negative pixels and runs dense `torch.cdist` against *all* N Gaussians. Complexity is O((M_pos+M_neg) N) time and intermediate memory; for N=500,000 and float32, the positive distance matrix alone is 2048*500000*4 = 4.096 GB (decimal) and negative 2.048 GB, before kNN/other allocations. A 10-second production pipeline needs a spatial index, candidate projection, or chunked/streaming kNN.
6. **[B]** Alignment regresses `1/rendered_depth` on normalized mono values with least squares, requires >=200 unchanged anchors and R²>=0.3; otherwise discards the entire camera. No robust fit, uncertainty weighting, or anchor spatial coverage test. Need log `failure_reason`, `anchor_pixels`, R², and `scale_a` by view.
7. **[B]** `trainer.update_cameras` uses `Random(seed).sample` for sparse views; view selection is not geometry-aware. `train.num_change_views` controls both change evidence and subsequent optimization view set, so changing view count simultaneously changes multiple variables.

### Mathematical interpretation / paper linkage
- **[D]** Existing ratio: `r_g = n_pos(g) / [n_pos_supported(g) + n_neg_supported(g)]`, not `n_pos(g)/n_physically_visible(g)`. One camera can contribute to both denominator terms. Proposed correction (NOT yet implemented): `V_g = {v | projected g in image, z>0, visible under alpha/depth occlusion test}`, `P_g = {v in V_g | positive evidence passes symmetric geometry gate}`, then `r_g = |P_g| / max(1, |V_g|)`. Keep positive and negative support as independent confidence values; do not confuse sampled evidence with visibility.
- **[C]** GS-DIFF, *From Pixels to Primitives* (2026), explicitly models anisotropic primitive drift and camera-geometry observability; however its main method compares **two reconstructed Gaussian sets**, whereas our task has one old set plus <=10 RGB. Transfer observability/anisotropic association ideas, not the entire algorithm. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07203
- **[C]** *Mastering Regional 3DGS* calibrates disparity via robust median + mean absolute deviation, `a=s(delta_GS)/s(delta_mono)`, `b=median(delta_GS)-a median(delta_mono)`, and initializes new Gaussians from calibrated depth (Eq. 6–7). Our immediate comparison should be float-depth + robust alignment, **not** blindly transplant its edited/unedited depth-ratio formula to physically changed scenes. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11263-026-02941-w

## 19:00–20:00 — Convergence causal audit: optimization vs topology vs camera exposure

**Research question:** Did the earlier 5-view changed-region PSNR improvement from 100 to 500 steps occur because densification began, or because existing parameters had more optimization/exposure? What else changes before the first new Gaussian can be born?

### Direct code facts and upstream gsplat source
1. **[B]** `configs/cl-splats.yaml` sets `densify_from_iter=500`, `densification_interval=100`, `densify_until_iter=15000`. `CLGaussians._build_strategy` passes these as `refine_start_iter`, `refine_every`, `refine_stop_iter`. `CLSplatsTrainer._train_step` passes **zero-based** `self._timestep_iter` into `gsplat.DefaultStrategy.step_post_backward`. The upstream strategy condition is **`step > refine_start_iter and step % refine_every == 0`**, not `step >= refine_start_iter`. Hence with current defaults, the **first possible duplication/splitting is on zero-based step 600, i.e., the 601st optimization iteration**, subject to actual gradients and version matching. At 100, 500, and even 600 training iterations, default strategy cannot have *grown* Gaussians. Primary upstream source: https://docs.gsplat.studio/main/_modules/gsplat/strategy/default.html (lines ~246–290). **This falsifies our earlier casual explanation that the 100→500 quality improvement was triggered by densification at 500.**
2. **[B]** No explicit ADD-specific Gaussian insertion appears in `prepare_timestep`: change masks are rendered and detected, `lifter.lift` selects existing rows as active, then the active subset is optimized. The only ordinary Gaussian birth is gsplat duplication/splitting at eligible refinement steps. Thus an entirely empty changed-space region has no new primitive center before the first growth, except by moving existing Gaussians; even at the first growth, children inherit locations near existing parents rather than a depth-lifted new-object surface. **Representation support is a plausible limitation, but not an explanation for the measured 100→500 difference by itself.**
3. **[B]** Topology can nevertheless **shrink before iteration 601**. `_constraint_prune_step` executes every `prune_every=50` (when active Gaussians and fitted primitives exist); even with `constraints.lambda_bound=0.0`, low-opacity **active** Gaussians below `densify_prune_opa=0.005` may be hard-pruned. Therefore 'no topology changes at all under 500' would be false; only 'no gsplat-driven Gaussian birth under 600' is supported. Its geometry-outlier hysteresis additionally requires `prune_consecutive=3`. Record births and prunes separately, not just net Gaussian count.
4. **[B]** `_build_strategy(cl_phase=True)` sets `prune_opa=0` and `reset_every=2**31-1` to protect frozen Gaussians. Upstream scale-based global pruning is gated by `step > reset_every`, so for normal update lengths the upstream global prune is not triggered. Earlier concern about inactive rows being removed by default strategy does **not** hold for the inspected CL phase under normal iteration counts. Do not report it as a bug.
5. **[B]** `_pop_random_train_camera` samples without replacement until the view stack empties, then refills. Consequently with V selected cameras and I steps, each view is exposed either `floor(I/V)` or `ceil(I/V)` times, average `E=I/V`. For V=5: I=100→20 exposures/view, I=500→100; for V=50: I=100→2. Comparing different view counts at the same I also changes per-view exposure. Random ordering remains a source of run-to-run variance.
6. **[B]** The `_train_step` PSNR/SSIM are computed from the **pre-update** `rendered` tensor after optimizer step (detached), not from a fresh post-update render. This gives a one-step lag and is training-view, not held-out, quality. Final held-out evaluation is performed separately when diagnostics and test cameras are enabled. Convergence conclusions must prioritize fixed held-out changed-region metrics.
7. **[B]** Default `lambda_bound=0`, so fitted primitives do not exert a geometric optimization constraint by default, although they can still be used by hard pruning. Also, gsplat strategy invokes `torch.cuda.empty_cache()` on refinement steps; if early refinement is enabled, instrument per-step latency spikes. The entire scene is still rendered each optimization step and dense Adam tensors exist for all Gaussians despite gradient masks: optimization latency need not scale with active count.

## 20:00–21:00 — Depth calibration and Gaussian birth: can initialization actually improve 100-step updates?

**Research question:** Does Mastering Regional 3DGS's depth-based initialization transfer to *physical* object insertion under <=5 valid RGB views, or can inaccurate depth anchors make the result worse? Can an alternative triangulation route outperform monocular depth?

### Verified code and paper evidence

1. **[B]** Inspected `clsplats/lifter/depth_anything_lifter.py` on the research branch. `estimate_depth` explicitly takes the Hugging Face pipeline's PIL `["depth"]`, casts uint8 values to float and divides by 255. `_align_relative_depth_with_stats` then fits `1/z_old = a * mono + b` via `torch.linalg.lstsq` on non-change pixels, rejecting views with fewer than 200 anchors or R² < 0.3. `configs/cl-splats.yaml` sets the depth model to `depth-anything/Depth-Anything-V2-Small-hf`, min positive views 2, depth tolerances 0.05 absolute and 0.05 relative.
2. **[C]** Mastering Regional 3DGS (IJCV, 12 Aug 2026), Eq. (6): `a=s(delta_GS)/s(delta_mono)`, `b=median(delta_GS)-a*median(delta_mono)`, with `s(delta)=mean(abs(delta-median(delta)))`; depth `=1/(a*delta_mono+b)`. Important mathematical distinction: **mean absolute deviation around median, not median absolute deviation**. The former is still sensitive to large outliers. Eq. (7) initializes new points using `z_old * z_mono(edited)/z_mono(unedited)`, unprojected in edited pixels. The paper reports visual editing improvement by ~300 vs ~1000 iterations, but not <=10s physical scene updates. Primary https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11263-026-02941-w .
3. **[B/D]** The current CL-Splats regression assumes the model output is affine to *inverse depth*. The pipeline calls it `predicted_depth`; whether it is best treated as depth, inverse depth or merely relative rank depends on actual model output semantics and per-view scaling. Test `x=mono` vs `x=1/mono` and sign/polarity, rather than silently forcing Eq. (6) to fit all views. This is an unverified modeling hypothesis, not a confirmed bug.
4. **[D]** R² may reject otherwise acceptable depth alignments when rendered anchor disparity has very low variance. Conversely high R² can coexist with systematic metric bias at occlusion boundaries. Validate median relative depth error and spatial coverage on *held-out unchanged anchors*, not just training-anchor R². DINO false negatives and inaccurate old rendering can contaminate anchor sets.
5. **[C]** EDGS (CVPR 2026) proposes dense two-view correspondence triangulation to initialize all Gaussians before optimization, avoiding progressive densification. This is an alternative geometric seed source to monocular depth. However its official README quotes ~10–20 s reconstruction plus ~5–10 s COLMAP, not an end-to-end <=10s local update. EDGS official root `LICENSE` was not found via GitHub connector on 2026-10-12, and the install path includes original 3DGS submodules: **commercial code clearance unverified**. Primary https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Kotovenko_EDGS_Eliminating_Densification_for_Efficient_Convergence_of_3DGS_CVPR_2026_paper.html ; https://github.com/CompVis/EDGS .
6. **[C]** Official Depth Anything V2 README lists Small (24.8M parameters) and advises native implementation rather than Hugging Face pipeline where OpenCV/Pillow upsampling differs. This is a separate interpolation confound if changing inference stacks; compare float vs uint8 **within the same pipeline first**. https://github.com/DepthAnything/Depth-Anything-V2 .

### Mechanism and failure cases

- **[D]** For trusted pixel `u=[x,y,1]^T`, depth `z_new` and known `K,T_wc`, create new center `X_world=T_wc[z_new K^-1 u;1]`, taking care with the project's transposed row-vector convention. Seed geometry *before* optimization, not via existing Gaussian movement.
- **[D]** Mastering Eq. (7) assumes comparable edited/unedited monocular outputs. In physical ADD, the old render depicts a background surface now occluded by the new object; separately normalized mono predictions may make the ratio unreliable. Direct calibrated new depth and two-view triangulation must be compared to Eq. (7), not assumed inferior.
- **[D]** Seed only where disparity positive and finite, change evidence reliable, and cross-view projection/depth agreement supports the point; reject points inconsistent with reliable unchanged pixels or strong occlusion. At fewer than two independent valid views, explicitly report weak geometric support and avoid asserting true 3D surface coverage.
- **[D]** Two independent bottlenecks now need separation: (i) whether depth is reliable enough to provide new 3D support, and (ii) whether extra support alone helps optimization. Compare geometrically informed seeds to **equal-count random seeds**, not only to no-seed baseline.

### Causal experiment specification

- **[E] D0–D4 depth calibration:** D0=current 8-bit/LS; D1=float/LS; D2=float/Eq.6 robust moments; D3=float/Huber IRLS; D4=float reciprocal-feature fit. Same 5 RGB, camera, old PLY, masks, weights and seed. R²-only success is insufficient.

## 21:00–22:00 — Novel-geometry support gate and anisotropic association

**Question:** Can the current lifter represent evidence on newly inserted surfaces where the old scene has no Gaussians?

**[B] Direct code:** `clsplats/lifter/depth_anything_lifter.py` associates new positive pixels exclusively with Euclidean top-8 **old Gaussian centers**. Candidates must satisfy both `||X_new-mu_old||/(||scale_old||+eps)<2.5` and `|z_old-z_new|<0.05+0.05*z_new`. It never creates new primitives. `CLGaussians.params.scales` are correctly activated linear scales; quaternions are available. Existing scale-norm gating ignores anisotropy and quaternion rotation. Euclidean top-k preselection can exclude a long, narrow Gaussian that would rank first by Mahalanobis distance.

**[D] Major new hypothesis:** For true ADD into previously empty space, *perfect* change masks and more views cannot rescue pixels for which no old Gaussian passes the radius and depth gates. This is a representational-support limitation distinct from ordinary sparse-view coverage or slow convergence. At old z=5m and new z=3m, the default depth tolerance is only 0.2m. This is conditional: nearby old floaters or boundaries may still pass. For supported old geometry, use rotation-aware covariance `Sigma=R(q)diag(s^2)R(q)^T` and `d_M^2=(X-mu)^T(Sigma+Sigma_noise)^-1(X-mu)`. A conservative candidate search must precede Mahalanobis reranking; reranking the existing top-8 cannot recover omitted candidates.

**[C] GS-DIFF:** The May 2026 paper compares two independently reconstructed GS sets, so its complete pipeline is not directly usable with old GS plus <=10 RGB. Its anisotropic drift/observability ideas are useful only as independent design inspiration. Official code license is CC BY-NC 4.0, verified from GitHub. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07203 and https://chumsy0725.github.io/GS-DIFF/ .

**[F] Reproducible CPU-only artifact:** `scripts/audit_lifting_support.py`, commit `fd820cc2af716980cc5c22cc5a5e10e38da2ff66`. The local synthetic test used an old z=5m plane of 21x21 Gaussians with s=(0.06,0.06,0.025). ## 22:00–23:00 — ADD/DELETE asymmetry, object-event routing, and historical priors

**Question:** Does the same depth-consistency gate that blocks novel ADD also block DELETE? What can CubifyGS and Cross-Temporal actually contribute to <=10 RGB / <=10 s?

**[B] Code:** The CL-Splats lifter backprojects DINO-positive pixels at the **new** aligned depth, selects Euclidean top-8 **old** Gaussian centers, then requires `|z_old_center - z_new| < 0.05 + 0.05 z_new` and local radius <2.5. Therefore, for clean removal where old foreground z=3m and revealed background z=5m, old foreground centers cannot get positive evidence (2m gap >0.30m), exactly as in clean ADD (old background z=5m, new object z=3m; 2m gap >0.20m). This is a **structural DELETE blind spot**, conditional on absence of alternative nearby floaters. `trainer.prepare_timestep` simply consumes the resulting old-Gaussian active mask; it has no ADD/DELETE/MOVE router. `_constraint_prune_step` removes only low-opacity or primitive-outlier **active** Gaussians, not vanished foreground objects directly. Thus deletion may fail even with a perfect 2D change mask. These are code-path implications, **not measured dataset failure rates**.

**[D] Minimal RGB-only geometric event evidence:** Let old GS depth be `z_o(u)`, calibrated new observation depth be `z_n(u)`, and `r(u)=z_n(u)-z_o(u)`. For confidence `q(u)` and uncertainty `tau(u)=tau_abs+tau_rel*z_n(u)+k*sigma_depth(u)`, mark `r< -tau` as **nearer-surface hypothesis** (ADD/MOVE-in), `r>tau` as **farther-surface hypothesis** (DELETE/MOVE-out/disocclusion), `|r|<=tau` with DINO change as appearance/small geometry hypothesis. **Depth sign is not semantic identity**; a MOVE requires matched nearer/farther components and a consistent rigid transform. Occlusion, pose drift, and mono-depth bias must produce an UNKNOWN/ABSTAIN outcome rather than forced deletion. For DELETE select **old GS Gaussians by projecting them into the positive 2D mask and checking old depth/visibility**, not by backprojecting at the revealed new background depth. For ADD initialize **new** Gaussians from calibrated new depth, with cross-view agreement. For APPEARANCE freeze geometry and optimize SH/color/opacity only. For MOVE reuse old asset via SE(3) if validated; else fallback to delete+add.

**[C] CubifyGS primary arXiv HTML:** https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28720 . The paper assumes a **continuous RGB-D stream plus known poses**, not <=10 RGB. Eqs. (4–5) ray-cast object-box occupancy using observed metric depth: observed depth before box = occluded, within box = hit, beyond box = free; a multi-frame existence score is decayed on high free-ray ratio while occlusion is low. Eq. (3): asset transform `mu'=R mu+t`, `Sigma'=R Sigma R^T`. Eqs. (9–11) define 3D-box-projected ROI and weighted pixel loss with focus weight gamma. Object retrieval uses a 12-canonical-view DINO bank (Eq. 2) and semantic cosine matching (Eq. 6); the paper requires an asset library and object proposals, neither present in current CL-Splats. The paper's 20 FPS maintenance on RTX 4090 must **not** be equated with a <=10 s full RGB-only update. Its ROI focus mechanism can be tested without object detectors, using the existing DINO change mask, but current `_photometric_loss` computes **scalar SSIM**, not a per-pixel DSSIM map. To implement CubifyGS Eq. (10) faithfully, add an SSIM map; for first causal test use ROI-weighted L1 alone. Normalize weights by their mean to avoid conflating ROI focus with global gradient magnitude / effective LR.

**[C] Cross-Temporal 3DGS:** AAAI 2026 official abstract confirms cross-temporal alignment, interference-based confidence initialization and progressive historical-prior optimization: https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/37217 . The precise confidence loss equation was **not independently verified from full paper** this hour, so do not attribute the following proposed formula to authors. **[D]** Proposed CL-Splats anchor: `L_anchor=sum_{g in active_old} w_g[lambda_x||mu_g-mu_g0||^2 + lambda_s||log(s_g/s_g0)||^2 + lambda_c||c_g-c_g0||^2]`. Apply only to uncertain **retained old** Gaussians near the ROI boundary; do NOT anchor DELETE targets or new ADD seeds. Inactive Gaussians are already exactly frozen by masked gradients + zeroed Adam moments, so a blanket historical prior is redundant and wastes latency.

**[C] Independent related alternative:** GaussianUpdate (ICCV 2025) explicitly separates removal/addition and uses sparse-point initialization, plus visibility-aware historical replay; its paper reports that densification alone was insufficient for newly added objects: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Zeng_GaussianUpdate_Continual_3D_Gaussian_Splatting_Update_for_Changing_Environments_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf .
